## 1.1 Introduction: From Single-Species to Multispecies Tactical Assessment

Traditional fisheries stock assessment relies on the single-species paradigm, in which each exploited population is modeled in isolation from its biological community. In single-species Virtual Population Analysis (VPA), Statistical Catch-at-Age (SCAA), or integrated assessment models such as Stock Synthesis ([Methot_Wetzel_2013_Stock_Synthesis.pdf](https://github.com/gustavdelius/multispecies_stock_assessment/blob/main/papers/Methot_Wetzel_2013_Stock_Synthesis.pdf)) and SAM ([Nielsen_Berg_2014_SAM_state_space_assessment_model.pdf](https://github.com/gustavdelius/multispecies_stock_assessment/blob/main/papers/Nielsen_Berg_2014_SAM_state_space_assessment_model.pdf)), all non-fishing mortality is aggregated into a single natural mortality rate $M_{s,a,t}$ that is frequently assumed constant across time and often across ages. Somatic growth (weight-at-age $w_{s,a,t}$) and maturity ogives ($\text{Mat}_{s,a,t}$) are typically entered as empirical covariates rather than state variables coupled to prey availability.

Decades of empirical and modeling work in the North Sea, Baltic Sea, Barents Sea, Iceland, Bering Sea, and Georges Bank have demonstrated that this single-species simplification breaks down in strongly interacting fish communities:
1. **Non-Stationary Predation Mortality ($M2$)**: Predation mortality inflicted by piscivorous fish, marine mammals, and seabirds typically exceeds fishing mortality $F$ on juvenile age classes and forage fish, and varies by orders of magnitude as predator biomasses fluctuate ([Lewy_Vinther_2004_SMS_stochastic_multispecies_model.pdf](https://github.com/gustavdelius/multispecies_stock_assessment/blob/main/papers/Lewy_Vinther_2004_SMS_stochastic_multispecies_model.pdf); [Trijoulet_etal_2019_Performance_multispecies_assessment_models.pdf](https://github.com/gustavdelius/multispecies_stock_assessment/blob/main/papers/Trijoulet_etal_2019_Performance_multispecies_assessment_models.pdf)). Ignoring time-varying $M2$ biases historical stock-recruitment relationships, reference points ($F_{\text{MSY}}$, $B_{\text{lim}}$), and short-term catch forecasts ([ICES_2023_WGSAM_report.pdf](https://github.com/gustavdelius/multispecies_stock_assessment/blob/main/papers/ICES_2023_WGSAM_report.pdf)).
2. **Prey-Driven Somatic Growth and Starvation**: Predators are food-limited across parts of their ontogeny. Depletion of forage fish (e.g., capelin *Mallotus villosus* in the Barents Sea and Icelandic waters, or sprat *Sprattus sprattus* in the Baltic Sea) directly reduces cod (*Gadus morhua*) feeding levels, somatic growth rates, condition factors, and reproductive output ([Stefansson_Palsson_1998_Structurally_detailed_statistical_models.pdf](https://github.com/gustavdelius/multispecies_stock_assessment/blob/main/papers/Stefansson_Palsson_1998_Structurally_detailed_statistical_models.pdf); [Taylor_Stefansson_2004_Gadget_cod_capelin_shrimp.pdf](https://github.com/gustavdelius/multispecies_stock_assessment/blob/main/papers/Taylor_Stefansson_2004_Gadget_cod_capelin_shrimp.pdf); [Hartvig_Andersen_Beyer_2011_Food_web_framework_size_structured.pdf](https://github.com/gustavdelius/multispecies_stock_assessment/blob/main/papers/Hartvig_Andersen_Beyer_2011_Food_web_framework_size_structured.pdf)).
3. **Ontogenetic Size-Structured Omnivory and Cannibalism**: In marine ecosystems, "who eats whom" is governed primarily by body size rather than taxonomic identity alone ([Ursin_1973_Prey_size_preferences_cod_dab.pdf](https://github.com/gustavdelius/multispecies_stock_assessment/blob/main/papers/Ursin_1973_Prey_size_preferences_cod_dab.pdf); [Andersen_2020_Size_based_theory_fisheries_advice.pdf](https://github.com/gustavdelius/multispecies_stock_assessment/blob/main/papers/Andersen_2020_Size_based_theory_fisheries_advice.pdf)). Large piscivores grow through five tosix orders of magnitude in body weight from egg to adult, transitioning from planktivory to benthivory/piscivory and cannibalism, which creates strong internal regulatory feedback loops ([Canales_Delius_Law_2020_Regulation_without_SRR.pdf](https://github.com/gustavdelius/multispecies_stock_assessment/blob/main/papers/Canales_Delius_Law_2020_Regulation_without_SRR.pdf)).
4. **Incompatibility of Single-Species $F_{\text{MSY}}$ Targets**: Attempting to simultaneously harvest all predator and prey stocks at their single-species $F_{\text{MSY}}$ targets is frequently biologically impossible because rebuilding predator stocks erodes prey yields and vice versa ([Pope_etal_2006_CSMS_size_based_community_model.pdf](https://github.com/gustavdelius/multispecies_stock_assessment/blob/main/papers/Pope_etal_2006_CSMS_size_based_community_model.pdf); [Pope_etal_2019_Comparing_multispecies_models_North_Sea.pdf](https://github.com/gustavdelius/multispecies_stock_assessment/blob/main/papers/Pope_etal_2019_Comparing_multispecies_models_North_Sea.pdf); [Andersen_2020_Size_based_theory_fisheries_advice.pdf](https://github.com/gustavdelius/multispecies_stock_assessment/blob/main/papers/Andersen_2020_Size_based_theory_fisheries_advice.pdf)).

## 1.2 The Complexity Spectrum: Strategic Models, Tactical Assessments, and MICE

Fisheries ecosystem models span a wide continuum of structural complexity, taxonomic resolution, and statistical rigor ([Plaganyi_etal_2014_MICE_models_of_intermediate_complexity.pdf](https://github.com/gustavdelius/multispecies_stock_assessment/blob/main/papers/Plaganyi_etal_2014_MICE_models_of_intermediate_complexity.pdf); [Collie_etal_2016_Ecosystem_models_finding_the_sweet_spot.pdf](https://github.com/gustavdelius/multispecies_stock_assessment/blob/main/papers/Collie_etal_2016_Ecosystem_models_finding_the_sweet_spot.pdf); [Karp_etal_2023_Increasing_uptake_of_multispecies_models.pdf](https://github.com/gustavdelius/multispecies_stock_assessment/blob/main/papers/Karp_etal_2023_Increasing_uptake_of_multispecies_models.pdf)):

```mermaid
flowchart LR
    A["Single-Species Assessments\n(SAM, SS3, MULTIFAN-CL)\nHigh statistical rigor\nZero trophic coupling"] --> B["Extended Single-Species\n(ESAM)\nPredator index covariates\non M or Q"]
    B --> C["Tactical Multispecies / MICE\n(SMS, CEATTLE, Gadget, Hydra,\nEcoState, Dynamic mizer)\nFormal likelihood fitting\nFocused food web (3-15 spp.)"]
    C --> D["Whole-Ecosystem / End-to-End\n(EwE, Atlantis, OSMOSE)\n30-100+ functional groups\nStrategic / MSE focus\nLimited formal parameter estimation"]
```

### 1.2.1 Models of Intermediate Complexity for Ecosystem Assessments (MICE)
Plagányi et al. ([Plaganyi_etal_2014_MICE_models_of_intermediate_complexity.pdf](https://github.com/gustavdelius/multispecies_stock_assessment/blob/main/papers/Plaganyi_etal_2014_MICE_models_of_intermediate_complexity.pdf)) and Collie et al. ([Collie_etal_2016_Ecosystem_models_finding_the_sweet_spot.pdf](https://github.com/gustavdelius/multispecies_stock_assessment/blob/main/papers/Collie_etal_2016_Ecosystem_models_finding_the_sweet_spot.pdf)) formalized the concept of **MICE** (Models of Intermediate Complexity for Ecosystem assessments) as the "sweet spot" for tactical fisheries management. As model complexity increases, **structural bias** (from omitting trophic interactions) decreases, whereas **parameter estimation uncertainty** (from fitting hundreds of poorly identified interaction rates to noisy data) increases rapidly ([Collie_etal_2016_Ecosystem_models_finding_the_sweet_spot.pdf](https://github.com/gustavdelius/multispecies_stock_assessment/blob/main/papers/Collie_etal_2016_Ecosystem_models_finding_the_sweet_spot.pdf), Fig. 1).

A model qualifies as a tactical multispecies assessment or MICE when it satisfies four operational criteria ([Plaganyi_etal_2014_MICE_models_of_intermediate_complexity.pdf](https://github.com/gustavdelius/multispecies_stock_assessment/blob/main/papers/Plaganyi_etal_2014_MICE_models_of_intermediate_complexity.pdf); [Karp_etal_2023_Increasing_uptake_of_multispecies_models.pdf](https://github.com/gustavdelius/multispecies_stock_assessment/blob/main/papers/Karp_etal_2023_Increasing_uptake_of_multispecies_models.pdf)):
1. **Context-Driven Taxonomic Scope**: It models only the core interacting species (typically $3\text{--}12$ commercial predator and prey stocks plus $1\text{--}3$ aggregated "other food" or resource pools) responsible for the majority of predation mortality and fishery yield.
2. **Formal Statistical Estimation**: Parameters are estimated by maximizing a formal likelihood (or posterior density) against empirical time series of commercial catches, scientific survey indices, age/size compositions, and stomach contents, rather than relying purely on literature plug-in values or manual tuning.
3. **Diagnostic and Uncertainty Quantification**: It provides standard stock assessment diagnostics (residual patterns, retrospective Mohn's $\rho$, profile likelihoods, Hessian/Laplace standard errors, or MCMC/SMC credible intervals).
4. **Operational Cadence**: It can be updated on the 1–5 year assessment cycle used by management bodies (e.g., ICES Working Group on Multispecies Assessment Methods [WGSAM] key runs supplying time-varying $M = M1 + M2$ matrices to single-species SAM assessments, or NOAA NPFMC Bering Sea CEATTLE runs).

## 1.3 Fundamental Axes of Structural Classification

Every multispecies stock assessment model can be positioned along three fundamental mathematical axes ([Stefansson_Palsson_1998_Structurally_detailed_statistical_models.pdf](https://github.com/gustavdelius/multispecies_stock_assessment/blob/main/papers/Stefansson_Palsson_1998_Structurally_detailed_statistical_models.pdf); [Andersen_Jacobsen_Farnsworth_2016_Theoretical_foundations_size_spectrum.pdf](https://github.com/gustavdelius/multispecies_stock_assessment/blob/main/papers/Andersen_Jacobsen_Farnsworth_2016_Theoretical_foundations_size_spectrum.pdf); [Pennino_etal_2026_Decade_of_mizer_review.pdf](https://github.com/gustavdelius/multispecies_stock_assessment/blob/main/papers/Pennino_etal_2026_Decade_of_mizer_review.pdf)):

### Axis 2: Direction of Trophic Feedback (One-Way vs. Two-Way Coupling)

A critical distinction in multispecies stock assessment—emphasized by Stefánsson & Pálsson ([Stefansson_Palsson_1998_Structurally_detailed_statistical_models.pdf](https://github.com/gustavdelius/multispecies_stock_assessment/blob/main/papers/Stefansson_Palsson_1998_Structurally_detailed_statistical_models.pdf)) and Andersen et al. ([Andersen_Jacobsen_Farnsworth_2016_Theoretical_foundations_size_spectrum.pdf](https://github.com/gustavdelius/multispecies_stock_assessment/blob/main/papers/Andersen_Jacobsen_Farnsworth_2016_Theoretical_foundations_size_spectrum.pdf))—is whether trophic interactions operate **one-way (top-down only)** or **two-way (top-down predation mortality + bottom-up bioenergetic growth/reproduction)**:

1. **One-Way Trophic Feedback ("Predation-Only" / Fixed-Ration Models)**:
   - Used in `MSVPA`, `SMS`, `LeMans`/`LeMaRns`, and standard modes of `MSCAA`/`Hydra`.
   - Predator abundance determines the predation mortality rate $M2$ experienced by prey:
     $$\text{Predator Abundance } N_{\text{pred}} \implies \text{Prey Mortality } M2_{\text{prey}}$$
   - However, **predator somatic growth rate, weight-at-age $w_{a,t}$, and per-capita total food consumption rate $R_{a,t}$ are independent of prey abundance**. Even if all modeled fish prey collapse, predators maintain their prescribed ration $R_{a,t}$ (implicitly compensating by consuming an inexhaustible "other food" pool $B_{\text{other}}$) and follow fixed growth curves.
   - *Consequence*: In forward projections under high forage-fish fishing mortality, one-way models predict that piscivores suffer **zero loss of growth or reproductive output** when their prey are fished down ([Jacobsen_etal_2016_Comparing_multispecies_model_predictions.pdf](https://github.com/gustavdelius/multispecies_stock_assessment/blob/main/papers/Jacobsen_etal_2016_Comparing_multispecies_model_predictions.pdf); [Pope_etal_2019_Comparing_multispecies_models_North_Sea.pdf](https://github.com/gustavdelius/multispecies_stock_assessment/blob/main/papers/Pope_etal_2019_Comparing_multispecies_models_North_Sea.pdf)).

2. **Two-Way Trophic Feedback ("Bioenergetic / Size-Spectrum" Models)**:
   - Used in `mizer`, `MULTSPEC`/`Gadget` (when configured with `g3a_grow_length_multspec` or `g3a_grow_weight_weightjones`), bioenergetic `CEATTLE`, and `Ecosim`/`EcoState`.
   - Every predation event simultaneously removes biomass from the prey cohort ($M2_{\text{prey}}$) **and** supplies assimilable energy to the predator cohort, driving somatic growth $g_{\text{pred}}$, energy reserves/condition $W_{\text{pred}}$, egg production $R_{p,\text{pred}}$, and (when energy intake falls below metabolic maintenance) starvation mortality $\mu_{s,\text{pred}}$:
     $$\text{Prey Abundance } N_{\text{prey}} \Longleftrightarrow \begin{cases} \text{Top-Down: Predation Mortality } M2_{\text{prey}} \\ \text{Bottom-Up: Predator Growth } g_{\text{pred}}, \text{ Reproduction } R_{p,\text{pred}}, \text{ Starvation } \mu_{s,\text{pred}} \end{cases}$$

### Axis 3: Calibration Paradigm (Deterministic VPA vs. Pattern-Oriented/History Matching vs. Integrated Statistical Likelihood)

1. **Deterministic / Iterative Backward VPA (`MSVPA`)**: Assumes catch-at-age and stomach contents are known without error; solves for historical $N_{a,t}$, $F_{a,t}$, and $M2_{a,t}$ via fixed-point iteration.
2. **Equilibrium / Pattern-Oriented / History Matching (`mizer` steady-state, `LeMaRns` SMC, `EwE`)**: Calibrates time-averaged catch or biomass against a steady-state reference period, or uses rejection/Sequential Monte Carlo sampling with implausibility metrics ([Spence_etal_2021_Quantifying_uncertainty_LeMaRns.pdf](https://github.com/gustavdelius/multispecies_stock_assessment/blob/main/papers/Spence_etal_2021_Quantifying_uncertainty_LeMaRns.pdf)) to identify ensembles of plausible parameter vectors.
3. **Integrated Maximum Likelihood / State-Space Assessment (`SMS`, `Gadget`/`gadget3`, `CEATTLE`, `MSCAA`/`Hydra`, `EcoState`)**: Formulates a differentiable forward process model coupled to explicit statistical observation models for each raw data stream (commercial catch-at-age/length, survey CPUE indices, stomach content proportions), optimizing the joint log-likelihood (or marginal Laplace likelihood in TMB) via automatic differentiation.

## 1.4 The Andersen & Ursin (1977) Theoretical Foundation and Livingston's (1983) Evaluation

Virtually all modern size- and age-structured multispecies models—including **MSVPA**, **SMS**, **Gadget**, **LeMans/LeMaRns**, **CSMS**, and **`mizer`**—trace their mathematical lineage to the landmark monograph by K. P. Andersen and Erik Ursin (1977), *"A Multispecies Extension to the Beverton and Holt Theory of Fishing, with Accounts of Phosphorus Circulation and Primary Production"*, Ursin's (1973) empirical analysis of prey size preferences in cod and dab ([Ursin_1973_Prey_size_preferences_cod_dab.pdf](https://github.com/gustavdelius/multispecies_stock_assessment/blob/main/papers/Ursin_1973_Prey_size_preferences_cod_dab.pdf)), and Patricia Livingston's (1983) mathematical evaluation and sensitivity analysis of the Andersen–Ursin equations for North Pacific fisheries ([Livingston_1983_Andersen_Ursin_model_evaluation.pdf](https://github.com/gustavdelius/multispecies_stock_assessment/blob/main/papers/Livingston_1983_Andersen_Ursin_model_evaluation.pdf)).

Because Andersen & Ursin (1977) is the shared ancestor of both statistical multispecies assessments (`SMS`, `MSCAA`, `CEATTLE`) and continuous size-spectrum models (`mizer`), examining its exact differential equations and parameter sensitivities—as documented by Livingston ([Livingston_1983_Andersen_Ursin_model_evaluation.pdf](https://github.com/gustavdelius/multispecies_stock_assessment/blob/main/papers/Livingston_1983_Andersen_Ursin_model_evaluation.pdf), pp. 2–11, Table 1)—clarifies how these two branches diverged.

### 1.4.1 Core Coupled Differential Equations of Andersen & Ursin (1977)
Andersen & Ursin (1977) extended the three basic Beverton & Holt (1957) differential equations for individual body weight $w_i(t)$, cohort abundance $N_i(t)$, and fishery yield $Y_i(t)$ of computational entity $i$ (a species-age group) ([Livingston_1983_Andersen_Ursin_model_evaluation.pdf](https://github.com/gustavdelius/multispecies_stock_assessment/blob/main/papers/Livingston_1983_Andersen_Ursin_model_evaluation.pdf), Eqs. 1–8):

1. **Bioenergetic Pütter / Generalized von Bertalanffy Somatic Growth**:
   The rate of food consumption $\frac{dR_i}{dt}$ by an individual of entity $i$ with body mass $w_i$ is proportional to its allometric absorbing surface $w_i^m$ and its dimensionless **feeding level** $f_i \in [0, 1]$ (capped in the full model at a critical feeding level $f_c = 0.44$):
   $$\frac{dR_i}{dt} = f_i \, h_i \, w_i^m \qquad (0 \le f_i \le f_c \le 1)$$
   Only a fraction $v_i = (1 - \alpha)\beta$ of consumed food is net-assimilated into tissue, where $\beta = 0.80$ is the digested fraction of food and $\alpha = 0.40$ is the fraction of digested energy lost in specific dynamic action / energy transfer costs (so $v_i = 0.48$ universally; [Livingston_1983_Andersen_Ursin_model_evaluation.pdf](https://github.com/gustavdelius/multispecies_stock_assessment/blob/main/papers/Livingston_1983_Andersen_Ursin_model_evaluation.pdf), Table 1, p. 11). Subtracting fasting metabolic catabolism $k_i w_i^n$ gives the individual weight ODE:
   $$\frac{dw_i}{dt} = v_i \frac{dR_i}{dt} - k_i w_i^n = (1 - \alpha)\beta \, f_i \, h_i \, w_i^m - k_i w_i^n$$
   where Andersen & Ursin (1977) estimated universal exponents $m = 0.56$ (or $m = 2/3$ in the simplified skeleton model) and $n = 0.83$ (or $n = 1$ in the skeleton model) across 81 fish species (Ursin 1967).

2. **Ursin's (1973) Lognormal Size-Preference Kernel and Suitability ($G_{ij}$)**:
   Analyzing thousands of stomach records of cod (*Gadus morhua*) and dab (*Limanda limanda*), Ursin ([Ursin_1973_Prey_size_preferences_cod_dab.pdf](https://github.com/gustavdelius/multispecies_stock_assessment/blob/main/papers/Ursin_1973_Prey_size_preferences_cod_dab.pdf), Eq. 2) demonstrated that predator $i$ of weight $w_i$ evaluates a potential prey item $j$ of weight $w_j$ according to a symmetric Gaussian distribution on the logarithmic predator-prey mass ratio $\ln(w_i / w_j)$:
   $$g_{ij}(w_i, w_j) = \exp\!\left( -\frac{\left(\ln\frac{w_i}{w_j} - \eta_i\right)^2}{2\sigma_i^2} \right)$$
   where $\eta_i$ is the preferred log predator-prey weight ratio ("a prey twice the preferred size is as acceptable as a prey half the preferred size"; [Livingston_1983_Andersen_Ursin_model_evaluation.pdf](https://github.com/gustavdelius/multispecies_stock_assessment/blob/main/papers/Livingston_1983_Andersen_Ursin_model_evaluation.pdf), p. 3, Fig. 1) and $\sigma_i$ is the standard deviation of diet breadth. Multiplying by species-specific behavioral/habitat vulnerability $\rho_{ij} \in (0, 1]$ gives total suitability:
   $$G_{ij} = \rho_{ij} \, g_{ij}(w_i, w_j)$$

3. **Available Food ($\Psi_i$), Search Volume ($q_i w_i^r$), and Feeding Level ($f_i$)**:
   Total suitable food biomass encountered by predator $i$ is $\Psi_i = \sum_j \Psi_{ij} = \sum_j G_{ij} w_j N_j$. The feeding level $f_i$ follows a Holling Type II saturation curve ([Livingston_1983_Andersen_Ursin_model_evaluation.pdf](https://github.com/gustavdelius/multispecies_stock_assessment/blob/main/papers/Livingston_1983_Andersen_Ursin_model_evaluation.pdf), Eq. 5–6):
   $$f_i = \frac{\Psi_i}{\Psi_i + Q_i}, \qquad Q_i = \frac{V \text{ (or } A\text{)}}{q_i \, w_i^r}$$
   where $V$ (pelagic water volume) or $A$ (demersal seabed area) scales the half-saturation food biomass $Q_i$, $r = 0.15$ is the universal search rate exponent, and $q_i$ is the search rate coefficient ($23\text{ m}^3\text{ g}^{-(1+r)}$ for pelagic habitat or $0.53\text{ m}^2\text{ g}^{-(1+r)}$ for demersal habitat; [Livingston_1983_Andersen_Ursin_model_evaluation.pdf](https://github.com/gustavdelius/multispecies_stock_assessment/blob/main/papers/Livingston_1983_Andersen_Ursin_model_evaluation.pdf), Table 1, p. 10).

4. **Cohort Mortality Partitioning ($F_i$, $\hat{M}_i$, $M2_i$) and Discrete Spawning**:
   Cohort numbers $N_i(t)$ decay according to ([Livingston_1983_Andersen_Ursin_model_evaluation.pdf](https://github.com/gustavdelius/multispecies_stock_assessment/blob/main/papers/Livingston_1983_Andersen_Ursin_model_evaluation.pdf), Eq. 2A, 8):
   $$\frac{dN_i}{dt} = -\left(F_i + \hat{M}_i + M2_i\right) N_i, \qquad \frac{dY_i}{dt} = F_i \, N_i \, w_i$$
   where predation mortality $M2_i$ inflicted on prey $i$ by all predator groups $j$ is the ratio of total biomass of $i$ consumed per unit time to the standing biomass $N_i w_i$ of $i$:
   $$M2_i = \frac{1}{N_i w_i} \sum_{j} \frac{dR_j}{dt} \, N_j \, \frac{\Psi_{ji}}{\Psi_j}$$
   Residual natural mortality $\hat{M}_i = M1_i + M4_i + M5_i + M6_i$ comprises density-dependent larval mortality $M1_i$ plus background disease ($M4_i \approx 0.03\text{ yr}^{-1}$), senescence ($M5_i$), and spawning stress ($M6_i$). At discrete spawning dates, mature age groups lose a weight fraction $\pi_i$ to release $E = \sum \pi_i(1-\pi_{\text{male}}) N_i w_i / \omega_i$ eggs of weight $\omega_i$, which hatch into age-0 recruits via a Beverton–Holt density-dependent survival function ([Livingston_1983_Andersen_Ursin_model_evaluation.pdf](https://github.com/gustavdelius/multispecies_stock_assessment/blob/main/papers/Livingston_1983_Andersen_Ursin_model_evaluation.pdf), Eq. 9, p. 13):
   $$E1 = E \left(\frac{C}{C + E}\right)$$

### 1.4.2 Nonlinear Parameter Sensitivity of the Andersen–Ursin System (`Livingston 1983`)
By perturbing each parameter of the 14-entity Andersen–Ursin skeleton model (phytoplankton, 3 zooplankton age classes, 4 herring age classes, 6 cod age classes) by $\pm 10\%$ and $\pm 20\%$ and fitting the nonlinear power-law response $y = \alpha x^\beta$ ([Livingston_1983_Andersen_Ursin_model_evaluation.pdf](https://github.com/gustavdelius/multispecies_stock_assessment/blob/main/papers/Livingston_1983_Andersen_Ursin_model_evaluation.pdf), pp. 13–27, Tables 4–6), Livingston discovered a striking hierarchy of parameter sensitivities:
- **Low Sensitivity ($|\beta| \le 0.5$, Table 4)**: Spawning fraction $\pi$, residual natural mortality $M1$, fish egg weights $\omega_2, \omega_3$, and herring fishing mortalities $F_6\text{--}F_8$ had weak, nearly linear effects on equilibrium numbers and body weights.
- **Moderate Sensitivity ($0.5 < |\beta| \le 1.5$, Table 5)**: Predator search rate scalar $Q$, density-dependent larval recruitment caps $C_2, C_3$, and adult cod fishing mortality $F_{14}$ had moderate effects; increasing $Q$ (i.e., reducing search efficiency $q_i$) lowered individual body weights $W_i$ ($\beta = -0.34$ to $-0.66$) while increasing prey numbers $N_i$ ($\beta = +0.12$ to $+0.53$) via reduced predation.
- **High, Strongly Nonlinear Sensitivity ($|\beta| > 1.5$, Table 6)**: The **anabolic growth and assimilation parameters** ($v, H_1, H_2, H_3, m$), catabolic exponent ($n$), and **mean prey size preference** ($\eta$) produced massive, strongly nonlinear responses across the food web (e.g., $\beta = +7.17$ for total weight with respect to anabolic exponent $m$, and $\beta = +2.39\text{ to }+3.37$ for cod weight with respect to assimilation efficiency $v$).
- **Mathematical Rationale**: In a two-way size-structured model, increasing anabolic growth ($H_i, m, v$) simultaneously (1) increases predator body mass $w_j$, (2) increases per-capita predator ration $h_j w_j^m$, and (3) shifts the predator's preferred prey size window $w_j e^{-\eta_j}$ across the prey size spectrum, creating a multiplicative trophic cascade across cohorts ([Livingston_1983_Andersen_Ursin_model_evaluation.pdf](https://github.com/gustavdelius/multispecies_stock_assessment/blob/main/papers/Livingston_1983_Andersen_Ursin_model_evaluation.pdf), pp. 26–27).

### 1.4.3 The Fundamental Divergence: How `SMS`/`MSVPA` vs. `mizer` Simplified Andersen & Ursin (1977)

```mermaid
flowchart TD
    AU["Andersen & Ursin (1977)\nFull Bioenergetic Multispecies Model\n83 coupled ODEs\nFeeding level f_j = Phi_j / (Phi_j + Q_j)\nPutter/von Bertalanffy growth: dw/dt = (1-alpha)beta f_j h w^n - k w^m"]
    AU -->|"Simplification 1: Helgason & Gislason (1979) / Pope (1979)\nAssume f_j = const (fixed ration R_j = f_j h_j w_j^n)\nFix weight-at-age w_{a,t} empirically"| MSVPA["MSVPA & SMS (Lewy & Vinther 2004)\nM2_{i,a} = sum_j (N_j R_j G_{ij}) / Phi_j\nExact match to stomach proportions!\nOne-way trophic feedback"]
    AU -->|"Simplification 2: Hartvig, Andersen & Beyer (2011)\nContinuous size-spectrum PDE\nExplicit search volume gamma_j w_j^q (so Q_j = h_j w_j^n / gamma_j w_j^q)\nDynamic growth g_j(w) & reproduction R_{p,j}"| MIZER["mizer (Scott et al. 2014)\nFull two-way bioenergetic feedback\nEmergent growth, maturity & M2\nHistorically lacked statistical time-series likelihoods"]
```

1. **The MSVPA / SMS Simplification (Helgason & Gislason 1979; Pope 1979; [Lewy_Vinther_2004_SMS_stochastic_multispecies_model.pdf](https://github.com/gustavdelius/multispecies_stock_assessment/blob/main/papers/Lewy_Vinther_2004_SMS_stochastic_multispecies_model.pdf))**:
   - In the late 1970s, the ICES Multispecies Working Group realized that Andersen & Ursin's full 83-ODE system had too many unknown bioenergetic parameters to estimate directly from fisheries data, **unless** one assumed that feeding level $f_j$ (and thus total consumption rate $R_j(w_j) = f_j h_j w_j^n$ and somatic growth $w_{a,t}$) was known externally from gastric evacuation studies and commercial weight-at-age sampling.
   - Under this assumption, the fraction of predator $j$'s diet consisting of prey $(i, w_i)$ is simply the ratio of suitable prey biomass to total suitable food:
     $$\text{Diet Proportion } D_{ij} = \frac{G_{ij} B_i}{\Phi_j}$$
   - Consequently, the instantaneous predation mortality rate $M2_i$ on prey $i$ simplifies to:
     $$M2_i = \sum_{j} \frac{\bar{N}_j \, R_j \, G_{ij}}{\Phi_j}$$
   - This brilliant simplification made $G_{ij}/\Phi_j$ **directly observable in stomach content data** (since stomach content weight proportions after evacuation correction estimate $D_{ij}$), launching the entire MSVPA and SMS assessment tradition. However, the price paid was the loss of dynamic growth feedback ($dw/dt$).

2. **The `mizer` Reformulation ([Hartvig_Andersen_Beyer_2011_Food_web_framework_size_structured.pdf](https://github.com/gustavdelius/multispecies_stock_assessment/blob/main/papers/Hartvig_Andersen_Beyer_2011_Food_web_framework_size_structured.pdf); [Scott_Blanchard_Andersen_2014_mizer_package.pdf](https://github.com/gustavdelius/multispecies_stock_assessment/blob/main/papers/Scott_Blanchard_Andersen_2014_mizer_package.pdf))**:
   - Thirty-four years after Andersen & Ursin (1977), Hartvig, Andersen & Beyer (2011) restored the full bioenergetic growth and reproduction equations of Andersen & Ursin within a continuous McKendrick–von Foerster PDE framework, replacing the arbitrary species-specific half-saturation constants $Q_j(w_j)$ with a mechanistic **volumetric search rate** $\gamma_j(w_j) = \gamma_j w_j^q$.
   - In `mizer`, the encounter rate of food is $E_j(w_j) = \gamma_j(w_j) \Phi_j(w_j)$, which implies an emergent half-saturation food density $Q_j(w_j) = \frac{h_j w_j^n}{\gamma_j w_j^q}$. All life-history parameters scale allometrically with individual weight $w$ and asymptotic species size $W_{\infty, j}$, reducing the parameter space from hundreds of cohort-specific constants to a compact set of scaling exponents and species traits.

## 1.5 Community-Level Dynamics and the Multispecies Jacobian Matrix

When comparing multispecies stock assessment models across these structural paradigms, Pope et al. ([Pope_etal_2006_CSMS_size_based_community_model.pdf](https://github.com/gustavdelius/multispecies_stock_assessment/blob/main/papers/Pope_etal_2006_CSMS_size_based_community_model.pdf); [Pope_etal_2019_Comparing_multispecies_models_North_Sea.pdf](https://github.com/gustavdelius/multispecies_stock_assessment/blob/main/papers/Pope_etal_2019_Comparing_multispecies_models_North_Sea.pdf)) and Jacobsen et al. ([Jacobsen_etal_2016_Comparing_multispecies_model_predictions.pdf](https://github.com/gustavdelius/multispecies_stock_assessment/blob/main/papers/Jacobsen_etal_2016_Comparing_multispecies_model_predictions.pdf)) introduced two unifying analytical diagnostics:

### 1.5.1 The Yield Vector and Jacobian Interaction Matrix ($\mathbf{J}$)
Let $\mathbf{F} = (F_1, F_2, \dots, F_S)^\top$ be the vector of long-term fishing mortality rates across $S$ species, and let $\mathbf{Y}(\mathbf{F}) = (Y_1, Y_2, \dots, Y_S)^\top$ and $\mathbf{SSB}(\mathbf{F}) = (\text{SSB}_1, \dots, \text{SSB}_S)^\top$ be the resulting equilibrium yield and spawning stock biomass vectors. Near a reference fishing vector $\mathbf{F}_0$, the response of the multispecies community is characterized by the **Jacobian matrix** ([Pope_etal_2019_Comparing_multispecies_models_North_Sea.pdf](https://github.com/gustavdelius/multispecies_stock_assessment/blob/main/papers/Pope_etal_2019_Comparing_multispecies_models_North_Sea.pdf), p. 5):

$$J_{ik} = \left.\frac{\partial \ln \text{SSB}_i}{\partial F_k}\right|_{\mathbf{F} = \mathbf{F}_0} \quad \text{or} \quad J^{(Y)}_{ik} = \left.\frac{\partial Y_i}{\partial F_k}\right|_{\mathbf{F} = \mathbf{F}_0}$$

- **Diagonal elements ($J_{ii} < 0$)**: Measure the direct single-species depletion + intraspecific cannibalism recovery response of species $i$ to its own fishing mortality $F_i$. Strong cannibalism (e.g., cod, whiting, hake) dampens $|J_{ii}|$ because fishing down large adults releases juveniles from cannibalistic $M2$.
- **Off-diagonal elements ($J_{ik}$ for $i \neq k$)**:
  - In **one-way models** (`SMS`, `LeMans`), $J_{\text{prey}, \text{pred}} > 0$ (fishing down a predator increases prey SSB by reducing $M2$), while $J_{\text{pred}, \text{prey}} \approx 0$ (fishing down a prey has zero direct effect on predator SSB, or only an indirect "prey-switching/shielding" effect if total suitable food $\Phi_{\text{pred}}$ changes cannibalism rates).
  - In **two-way bioenergetic models** (`mizer`, `Gadget`, `Ecosim`), $J_{\text{pred}, \text{prey}} < 0$ because fishing down forage fish reduces predator feeding level $f_{\text{pred}}$, slowing predator somatic growth $g_{\text{pred}}$ and lowering reproductive output $R_{p,\text{pred}}$.

### 1.5.2 Quadratic Approximation of Total Multispecies Yield
Pope et al. ([Pope_etal_2006_CSMS_size_based_community_model.pdf](https://github.com/gustavdelius/multispecies_stock_assessment/blob/main/papers/Pope_etal_2006_CSMS_size_based_community_model.pdf); [Pope_etal_2019_Comparing_multispecies_models_North_Sea.pdf](https://github.com/gustavdelius/multispecies_stock_assessment/blob/main/papers/Pope_etal_2019_Comparing_multispecies_models_North_Sea.pdf)) showed that across widely different multispecies models of the North Sea (`SMS`, `Gadget`, `EwE`, `LeMans`, `mizer`, `CSMS`, `CSM`), the equilibrium yield of species $i$ as a function of the fishing mortality vector $\mathbf{F}$ is well approximated by a coupled quadratic form (a multispecies generalization of the Schaefer surplus production parabola):

$$Y_i(\mathbf{F}) \approx a_i F_i + \sum_{k=1}^{S} b_{ik} F_i F_k$$

In matrix notation, total community yield $Y_{\text{tot}}(\mathbf{F}) = \sum_{i=1}^S Y_i(\mathbf{F}) = \mathbf{a}^\top \mathbf{F} + \mathbf{F}^\top \mathbf{B} \mathbf{F}$. An unconstrained community-wide MSY exists at $\mathbf{F}_{\text{MSY}} = -\frac{1}{2}(\mathbf{B} + \mathbf{B}^\top)^{-1}\mathbf{a}$ only if the symmetric part of the interaction matrix $\frac{1}{2}(\mathbf{B} + \mathbf{B}^\top)$ is negative definite; in practice, strong predation terms $b_{ik} > 0$ often make the unconstrained optimum corner-valued ("fish out the predators to maximize forage fish yield"), necessitating conservation constraints ($B_i \ge B_{\text{lim}, i}$) or value-weighted objective functions ([Pope_etal_2019_Comparing_multispecies_models_North_Sea.pdf](https://github.com/gustavdelius/multispecies_stock_assessment/blob/main/papers/Pope_etal_2019_Comparing_multispecies_models_North_Sea.pdf)).
